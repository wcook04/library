Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations

  • Levent Alpöge
  • Tristan Buckmaster

Explanatory reading edition prepared by Will Cook with AI assistance, 22 November 2026. Appendix C retains the authors’ account of their computation. Based on the manuscript of 20 November 2026 (revision ad53419).

Suppose the unforced three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations have a smooth nonzero whole-space solution u∗u^*u∗ before time TTT that repeats on shrinking scales up to an orthogonal transformation and satisfies ∣u∗(t,x)∣≤C/(∣x∣+T−t)|u^*(t,x)|\le C/(|x|+\sqrt{T-t})∣u∗(t,x)∣≤C/(∣x∣+T−t​). From this singular flow we construct finite-energy solutions with smooth divergence-free data, compactly supported on R3\mathbb R^3R3 or periodic on T3\mathbb T^3T3. Their rescaled velocity gradients converge to the profile gradient in L2L^2L2, globally on R3\mathbb R^3R3 and on expanding balls in similarity coordinates on the torus. The whole-space gradient norm in L2L^2L2 and the torus H1H^1H1 norm grow between positive multiples of the inverse fourth root of the time remaining. We cut off the whole-space datum and adjust it in finitely many directions, without a spectral stability assumption. A second theorem constructs the required profile from a family of approximations satisfying explicit inequalities. Alpöge and Buckmaster report verifying sufficient finite-data conditions by interval arithmetic. Their cited release contains the conditional proofs and omits the data and interval calculation (Subsection 1.5).
