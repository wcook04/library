Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations

  • Levent Alpöge
  • Tristan Buckmaster

Adapted from the paper by Alpöge and Buckmaster; every change is marked. Read the authors’ paper. Reading copy, 85 pages · PDF with change record.

A nonzero smooth Navier–Stokes profile that recurs in logarithmic time up to a linear isometry and obeys ∣W(s,y)∣≤C(1+∣y∣)−1|W(s,y)|\le C(1+|y|)^{-1}∣W(s,y)∣≤C(1+∣y∣)−1 gives finite-time blow-up for the unforced three-dimensional equations. The initial velocities are smooth and divergence-free, compactly supported on R3\mathbb R^3R3 and periodic on T3\mathbb T^3T3. A correction in a fixed finite-dimensional space of whole-space data allows the solution to follow the profile without a spectral stability assumption. The norm ∥∇u(t)∥L2(R3)\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^3)}∥∇u(t)∥L2(R3)​ is comparable to (1−t)−1/4(1-t)^{-1/4}(1−t)−1/4 up to time 111, and ∥uper(t)∥H1(T3)\|u_{\mathrm{per}}(t)\|_{H^1(\mathbb T^3)}∥uper​(t)∥H1(T3)​ to (T−t)−1/4(T-t)^{-1/4}(T−t)−1/4 up to some 0<T≤10<T\le10<T≤1. The rescaled velocity gradients converge to those of the profile in L2L^2L2, on all of R3\mathbb R^3R3 in the first case and on expanding balls in the second. A second theorem selects one member of an approximate family satisfying the certificate bounds and corrects it to an exact periodic profile. Alpöge and Buckmaster report verifying the conditions on their particular certificate by interval arithmetic. With this input the construction disproves (A) and (B) of Fefferman’s formulation of the Millennium Prize problem and realizes (C) and (D) with zero force. The certificate data and verification calculation do not accompany this edition (Subsection 1.5).
