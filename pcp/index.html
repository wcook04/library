Constant-query verification by algebraic encoding

Explanatory reconstruction prepared by Will Cook with AI assistance · 16 November 2026.

Algebraic construction: Amireddy, Behera, Srinivasan, Sudan and Willumsgaard. Lean formalization: Troy Lee.

Abstract

The PCP theorem gives every language in NP a polynomial-time verifier with logarithmic randomness, constantly many proof-bit queries, perfect completeness and soundness error at most one half. We explain Troy Lee’s Lean formalization of the algebraic proof of Amireddy, Behera, Srinivasan, Sudan and Willumsgaard. In the soundness proof the verifier’s checks force four fixed nearby polynomials to encode a proper colouring. Its central step is local correction at prescribed points, where closeness on average gives no guarantee; we bound the error by the corrector’s own rejection probability.

Remark (formalization). Lee proves Theorem 1.1 in this form, for a verifier implemented in a concrete machine model; surplus random bits may be ignored. [ABSSW26, Theorem 2.2] states O(log⁡n)O(\log n)O(logn) randomness.

The theorem is due to Arora, Lund, Motwani, Sudan and Szegedy [ALMSS98], building on Arora and Safra [AS98]. In a field of size 2t2^t2t, a field value takes ttt bits, and the coefficient vector of a line polynomial of degree at most ddd takes (d+1)t(d+1)t(d+1)t bits. Counting either as one symbol, as the point-versus-line test of [ALMSS98] does, does not establish constant bit-query complexity, and binary encoding of whole coefficient vectors can need quasipolynomial proof length. The construction maps a line polynomial of degree below bcb^cbc to one of total degree at most ccc in cbcbcb variables, ccc fixed, and Hadamard-encodes single field values for one-bit queries, without PCP composition [ABSSW26, §1].

If the low-degree tests reject rarely, four nearby polynomials have encodings close to the received tables; the analysis chooses them and the verifier never computes them. The other checks force them to encode a proper colouring, the difficulty being local correction at prescribed points. The construction and analysis are those of [ABSSW26]; Lee [Lee26] formalized them, added explicit constants and a weaker field-size hypothesis for the low-degree test over FFF, and identified the error in version 1 of [ABSSW26]. We contribute an explanation of the correction bound and the soundness argument with those constants.

Sections 2 to 7 give notation, the encodings and the proof, testing, correction, soundness, resources and the converse. The full argument proves the testing results; the worked exposition gives examples.
