Abstract. We prove that if EEE satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition at base two and VVV admits a summable positive divisor cover, then ∑a∈A(ba−1)−1\sum_{a\in A}(b^a-1)^{-1}∑a∈A​(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every infinite A⊆E∪VA\subseteq E\cup VA⊆E∪V and every integer b≥2b\ge2b≥2. The two hereditary support classes are incomparable, each contains supports of divergent reciprocal sum, and their unions can lie in neither class. The proof compares a multiple of the series with an integer and averages the error first over multiples of a modulus and then over dyadic lengths. The second average restores the factor 1/a1/a1/a that incomplete residue periods lose, and because both criteria run through the same average, a single index makes both errors small at once. Irrationality for arbitrary infinite support at base two remains open.

The displacement is a positive real number and may exceed 111, so it should be distinguished from a fractional part; Erdős also proposed working with fractional parts of powers times the value of the series [7, p. 226].

To make the displacement small we take NNN to be a multiple of a modulus. Divisibility then cancels the finitely many exponents that divide the modulus, and the remaining terms have small means over a complete period when their gcd with the modulus is large. The bound for an incomplete period lacks the factor 1/a1/a1/a, and averaging over dyadic lengths restores it: an exponent enters the truncated error only once the sampled range reaches it, and the reciprocals of the later lengths form a geometric tail bounded by a multiple of 1/a1/a1/a. Exponents beyond the sampled range are estimated separately. Because every bound depends explicitly on the modulus, both criteria can share one average.

Sections 2–4 prove the three criteria, and Sections 5–6 separate their support classes and describe the limits of the method. The appendices give the direct proof for reciprocal-summable supports, the exact denominators of finite subseries, and tests for whether a prescribed rational number is a subseries sum; these tests decide neither 1/21/21/2 nor 1/211/211/21.
