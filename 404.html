  1. Finite-Time Singularity Formation for the Three-Dimensional Euler Equations

    By Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster and Matei P. Coiculescu

    Constructs smooth periodic initial data for the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations whose solution blows up in finite time, with the velocity bounded and the vorticity at the origin tending to infinity.

  2. Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations↗

    By Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster

    Proves that there are smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free initial data on ℝ³, and smooth divergence-free data on the torus, whose solutions of the unforced three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations blow up in finite time, with the profile obtained from a numerical certificate the authors will release.

  3. Which sums of 1/(2ⁿ − 1) are irrational?↗

    By Will Cook, using AI

    Proves that the sum of 1/(2ᵃ − 1) over an infinite set A is irrational when A satisfies a weighted summability condition, has a summable divisor cover, or lies in a union of one set of each kind, at every integer base b ≥ 2; Erdős’s question for every infinite A stays open.

    Three papers on Erdős problem #257, made by Will Cook using AI. No independent mathematician has reviewed them.

    On its current site, Erdős 257 Reader.

  4. Why the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick order parameter has no gaps↗

    By OpenAI (the proof and its Lean) and Will Cook, using AI (the three papers)

    A minimising Parisi order parameter of the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick spin glass has no gaps: it increases across every interval in [0, 1), and its derivative is positive between 0 and 1.

    The proof and its Lean are OpenAI’s. Hong-Bin Chen first proved that a minimising order parameter has no gaps.

  5. A nearly quartic separation between randomized and quantum query complexity↗

    By OpenAI

    For every power below four there is a total Boolean function whose randomized query complexity exceeds that power of its quantum query complexity, and four is the best possible exponent; Lean checks the optimal exponent with no hypothesis.

    On its current site, Quartic Reader.

  6. Constant-query verification by algebraic encoding↗

    By Will Cook, with AI assistance (explanatory reconstruction)

    The PCP theorem: every language in NP has a polynomial-time verifier with logarithmic randomness, constantly many proof-bit queries, perfect completeness and soundness error at most one half, by the algebraic proof of Amireddy, Behera, Srinivasan, Sudan and Willumsgaard.

    Also

    • Troy Lee’s reading guide

    Links 177 phrases to 101 Lean declarations; 23 of the phrases state a result.

    Comparator accepted the forward theorem, its converse and the equivalence with the reference NP class.

    Original algebraic proof by Amireddy, Behera, Srinivasan, Sudan and Willumsgaard. Lean formalization by Troy Lee.

    On its current site, Proof Reader.

  9. The minimum of the coupled spring potential↗

    By Bjørn Kjos-Hanssen (the Lean) and AI agents under Will Cook’s direction (the page)

    The coupled spring potential U(x₀, x₁) = x₀² + x₀x₁ + x₁² has a local minimum at the origin, proved in PhyslibAlpha by a general second-derivative test.
