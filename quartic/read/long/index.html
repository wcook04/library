OpenAI’s nearly quartic query separation: a complete proof reconstruction

Written by AI systems under the direction of Will Cook · November 2026

Abstract. Let R(f)R(f)R(f) and Q(f)Q(f)Q(f) denote the randomized and quantum query complexities of a Boolean function, with error at most 1/31/31/3. We reconstruct OpenAI’s proof that four is the least exponent α\alphaα for which R(f)=O((1+Q(f))α)R(f)=O((1+Q(f))^\alpha)R(f)=O((1+Q(f))α) holds for every total Boolean function. The input consists of mmm columns. A column wins if its bit string consists of ones and its correctly addressed cell contains valid circuit transcripts and the row of a zero in each other column. A quantum algorithm compares columns and verifies only the candidates on a short list. On the classical side, the algorithm must specify the entire address of a cell before reading even one of its bits. We choose the address bits as answers to independent instances of a partial function. With k≥2k\ge2k≥2 blocks in each auxiliary input and kkk fixed, the quantum algorithm uses at most m\sqrt mm​ times a polylogarithmic factor in queries. Randomized algorithms require at least order m2−1/k/(log⁡m)2m^{2-1/k}/(\log m)^2m2−1/k/(logm)2 queries. We give an alternative proof of the required kkk-fold Forrelation lower bound by Gaussian integration by parts. A separate quantitative argument, using estimates uniform in kkk, gives a single family with separation exponent tending to four.
