  1. Finite-Time Singularity Formation for the Three-Dimensional Euler Equations

    By Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster and Matei P. Coiculescu

    Constructs smooth periodic initial data for the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations whose solution blows up in finite time, with the velocity bounded and the vorticity at the origin tending to infinity.

  2. Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations

    By Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster

    Proves that there are smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free initial data on ℝ³, and smooth divergence-free data on the torus, whose solutions of the unforced three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations blow up in finite time, with the profile obtained from a numerical certificate the authors will release.

  3. Which sums of 1/(2ⁿ − 1) are irrational?

    By Will Cook

    Proves that the sum of 1/(2ᵃ − 1) over an infinite set A is irrational when A satisfies a weighted summability condition, has a summable divisor cover, or lies in a union of one set of each kind, at every integer base b ≥ 2; Erdős’s question for every infinite A stays open.

  4. Why the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick order parameter has no gaps

    By OpenAI (the proof and its Lean) and Will Cook, using AI (the three papers)

    A minimising Parisi order parameter of the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick spin glass has no gaps: it increases across every interval in [0, 1), and its derivative is positive between 0 and 1.

  5. Reconstructing OpenAI’s nearly quartic query separation

    By AI systems under the direction of Will Cook

    For every power below four there is a total Boolean function whose randomized query complexity exceeds that power of its quantum query complexity, and four is the best possible exponent. Three accounts of OpenAI’s proof, each linked phrase by phrase to the Lean, which checks the optimal exponent with no hypothesis.

  6. Constant-query verification by algebraic encoding

    By Will Cook, using AI

  9. The minimum of the coupled spring potential
