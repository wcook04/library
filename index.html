Lead papers

Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes and Euler equations, each read beside the authors’ own Lean.

  1. Credit: AnthropicAuthors’ paper · Adapted edition

    Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations

    Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster

    Proves that there are smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free initial data on ℝ³, and smooth divergence-free data on the torus, whose solutions of the unforced three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations blow up in finite time, with the profile obtained from a numerical certificate the authors will release.

  2. Credit: AnthropicAuthors’ paper · Adapted edition

    Finite-Time Singularity Formation for the Three-Dimensional Euler Equations

    Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster and Matei P. Coiculescu

    Constructs smooth periodic initial data for the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations whose solution blows up in finite time, with the velocity bounded and the vorticity at the origin tending to infinity.

  1. Credit: the authorsFormalizer’s guide · Companion explanations

    Constant-query verification by algebraic encoding

    The PCP theorem: every language in NP has a polynomial-time verifier with logarithmic randomness, constantly many proof-bit queries, perfect completeness and soundness error at most one half, by the algebraic proof of Amireddy, Behera, Srinivasan, Sudan and Willumsgaard.

    Reconstructing OpenAI’s nearly quartic query separation

Will Cook’s own research

Eight problems posed by Paul Erdős, researched with AI agents and written up through the funnel in the video above: a short paper and a longer record for each, with Lean proofs of selected statements. Erdős #257 also has a reader here. The answer for #1041 refutes one formal statement; the other seven problems remain open. No independent mathematician has reviewed these papers.

  1. Will Cook’s researchOriginal research

    Which sums of 1/(2ⁿ − 1) are irrational?

    Proves that the sum of 1/(2ᵃ − 1) over an infinite set A is irrational when A satisfies a weighted summability condition, has a summable divisor cover, or lies in a union of one set of each kind, at every integer base b ≥ 2; Erdős’s question for every infinite A stays open.

    Erdős #243: Cubic-rate irrationality and reciprocal tails↗

    Proves that a strictly increasing integer sequence with a_n²/a_(n+1) = 1 + 3/n + o(n⁻³) has an irrational sum of reciprocals, and the same for every nonintegral regular rate above one, with Lean checks. The unrestricted Sylvester-tail question remains open.

    Erdős #68: Factorial-denominator irrationality↗
